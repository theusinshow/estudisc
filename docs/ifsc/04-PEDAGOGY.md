# 04 — Pedagogy

Status: **Accepted**
Source of truth for: teaching rules and evidence semantics.

## Core rule

Explain the minimum needed to enable an attempt, observe the attempt, provide specific feedback, then decide what to teach or practice next.

Passive reading is not the default learning loop.

## Active recall

For review/retrieval, ask before showing the recap.

`attempt recall → feedback → compact recap if necessary`

## Scaffolding

Default progression:

`context → concept → worked example → guided practice → independent practice → transfer`

Support fades as learner evidence improves.

## Hint ladder

- Hint 1: conceptual direction.
- Hint 2: identify first step.
- Hint 3: partial solution.
- Solution: full resolution where mode permits.

Hint use is recorded and reduces evidence independence. It is not presented as punishment.

## Feedback

Feedback must answer:

1. what happened;
2. why;
3. what to do next when remediation is needed.

A correct intermediate calculation paired with a wrong final interpretation may produce positive evidence for one Concept and negative evidence for another.

## Learning-error taxonomy

`CONCEPTUAL, PROCEDURAL, CALCULATION, INTERPRETATION, PREREQUISITE, DISTRACTOR, ATTENTION, MEMORY, UNKNOWN`

Classification precedence:

1. deterministic rule;
2. known distractor pattern;
3. explicit student self-report;
4. AI inference;
5. UNKNOWN.

AI classification includes confidence and is not authoritative.

## Remediation

Repeated error pattern:

`feedback → short alternate explanation/example → micro exercise → equivalent retry`

Persistent failure may trigger prerequisite checks.

## Difficulty

- `FOUNDATION`
- `DIRECT`
- `APPLIED`
- `IFSC`
- `CHALLENGE`

Difficulty increases through reasoning and representation complexity, not merely larger numbers.

## Interleaving

After initial acquisition, practice mixes:

- current Concepts;
- older Concepts;
- known weakness;
- transfer/IFSC-style tasks.

## Learning modes

| Capability | LEARN | PRACTICE | REVIEW | EXAM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hints | yes | limited | limited | no |
| Tutor | yes | yes | yes | no |
| Immediate feedback | yes | yes | yes | no |
| Retry | yes | limited | limited | no |
| Solution | allowed | after attempt | after attempt | only after finalization |

## Lesson kinds

- `CORE` — substantial new content, typically 20–40 min.
- `COMPACT` — smaller complementary unit, typically 8–18 min.
- `PRACTICE` — evidence/consolidation, typically 15–30 min.
- `SYNTHESIS` — integrates multiple Concepts, typically 20–40 min.

## Exit ticket

A CORE Lesson normally ends with:

1. direct question;
2. applied question;
3. transfer/context question.

Exit ticket is immediate evidence, not sufficient by itself for Mastered.

## Mastery principles

- Lesson completion is never mastery.
- Mastery belongs to atomic Concepts.
- delayed retrieval and transfer are important for strong states;
- passage of time primarily affects retention/review state rather than arbitrarily deleting prior mastery;
- policy versions remain explainable and recalculable.

## Gamification

Learning feedback and next action appear before XP/badges. Gamification never owns mastery, review or Planner decisions.
